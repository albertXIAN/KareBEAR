# EVIDENCE.md — KareBEAR V0 Evidence, Design Decisions, Limitations & Proposed Tests

This document maps the inputs behind the **KareBEAR V0 Clickable Prototype** using the five-part traceability structure:

$$\text{Source} \rightarrow \text{Finding or Recommendation} \rightarrow \text{Design Decision} \rightarrow \text{Limitation} \rightarrow \text{Proposed Test}$$

To prevent conflating unvalidated choices with clinical or empirical facts, entries are separated into five categories:
1. **Clinical & Health Literacy Research / Published Guidance**
2. **Stakeholder Feedback (Earlier Community Partner Co-Creation Sessions)**
3. **Technical & Implementation Advice**
4. **Accessibility Standards (WCAG 2.2 AA)**
5. **Working Assumptions & Design Choices Requiring Evaluation** (including ages 7–12, the Kare bear mascot, colour preferences, and sentence-length targets)

> **Boundary Rule:** All research notes, evaluation tasks, and implementation details in this file are kept strictly outside child-facing screens.

## 1. Clinical & Health Literacy Research / Published Guidance

| Source | Finding or Recommendation | Design Decision | Limitation | Proposed Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SickKids (AboutKidsHealth)** — *Cast care: Arm or leg cast* (`https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/cast-care-arm-or-leg-cast`) | Non-waterproof casts (plaster or standard fiberglass) must be kept completely dry, must never have objects inserted inside to scratch itching skin, and require urgent assessment if circulation/nerve warning signs appear (tingling, numbness, coldness, colour change, or worsening pain). | Grounded the three recovery points in SickKids non-waterproof cast guidance, linked directly to the full SickKids article on S4, S8, and the global footer, and explicitly stated that the demo covers a **non-waterproof cast** using **three selected learning examples**, not a complete care plan. | Simplified child-friendly sentences (`⚑ Needs clinical review`) have not yet been formally signed off by hospital clinical reviewers, and excluded topics (elevation, finger movement, cast damage, pain medication) are omitted from the 3-step demo. | Clinical SME review comparing each `⚑` child line and `"More about this"` expansion against the SickKids source text for accuracy and completeness before any clinical pilot. |
| **Paediatric discharge communication & teach-back literature** (e.g., AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit; paediatric cast-care recall studies) | Recognition in multiple-choice questions does not guarantee that a child or caregiver can articulate care actions in their own words; pairing recognition checks with an own-words explanation improves recall and surfaces misunderstandings. | Renamed multiple-choice activities to **“comprehension checks”**; used the interleaved sequence **Learn (S6) → Comprehension Check (S7) → Supportive Feedback / Revisit → Next Point**; added an explicit, unrecorded prompt on each step and on S8 inviting the child to explain the point to their caregiver in their own words. | V0 does not record audio or evaluate spoken explanations automatically, so explanation quality relies on caregiver engagement during the session. | Observational workshop test: record how often child-caregiver dyads pause to converse at the `"Tell your grown-up in your own words"` prompt and whether caregivers feel confident guiding that conversation. |
| **Acute paediatric injury safety framing** | Suggesting that a family wait until the next day after an acute arm injury to visit the hospital could model unsafe care-seeking behaviour. | Framed the Journey 1 scenario (Task A) as a child **already at the hospital clinic or emergency department waiting for assessment and cast fitting**, rather than waiting until tomorrow after an injury. | Does not test pre-arrival home preparation for scheduled elective procedures (such as planned day surgery). | Post-task comprehension interview with parents to confirm the scenario framing feels realistic for an urgent fracture clinic or emergency department wait. |

## 2. Stakeholder Feedback (Earlier Community Partner Co-Creation Sessions)

| Source | Finding or Recommendation | Design Decision | Limitation | Proposed Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Community partner co-creation sessions (pre-prototype)** | Caregivers wanted visibility and control over what their child sees first, and wanted side-by-side access to the original hospital wording so they could trust simplified explanations. | Created S4 (**Grown-up review**) before the child handover (S5), with an expandable **“See original wording & SickKids source”** toggle on every point and caregiver controls for supplemental detail (`"Include 'More about this' extra detail"`) and presentation preferences (`"Explain in your own words"` prompts and `"Listen"` read-aloud button). | Caregiver approval is a trust-based session step with no authentication barrier, and partners have not yet tested this specific V0 interface. | Task B usability observation: measure whether caregivers understand that approval means **permission to show the child the demonstration content** (not clinical validation) and whether they use the original-wording toggle. |
| **Community partner co-creation + clinical safety tension** | Partners requested caregiver control over content, but allowing a caregiver to untick core cast care rules or neurovascular warning signs could hide essential safety instructions. | Kept all three core demo care points locked ON with the neutral label **“Always included in this demonstration”** (replacing `"Your care team marked this as important"` so V0 does not imply clinician configuration), while allowing caregivers to toggle supplemental detail and presentation preferences. | Caregivers who wish to skip a topic entirely for an anxious child cannot hide any of the three core demonstration steps. | Post-Task B interview: ask caregivers how they interpret `"Always included in this demonstration"` and whether controlling supplemental detail satisfies their need for customization. |

## 3. Technical & Implementation Advice

| Source | Finding or Recommendation | Design Decision | Limitation | Proposed Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical advisor consultation** | Early-stage usability prototypes should avoid live LLM generation, speech recognition grading, backend patient storage, or authentication so clinical wording remains deterministic and privacy-safe during testing. | Built KareBEAR V0 as a deterministic, single-page React + TypeScript application with all copy in `src/data/demoContent.ts`, pure state transitions in `src/utils/demoState.ts`, in-memory session state that resets on refresh, a **Reset Demo** button, and optional browser `speechSynthesis` for `"Listen"`. | In-memory state is lost if the browser tab is refreshed mid-session; browser TTS voice quality varies across operating systems. | Cross-device technical check on tablet (1024×768) and laptop (1366×768 / 1440×900) verifying zero network calls for content, clean state resets, and graceful fallback when TTS is unavailable. |
| **State & attempt tracking specification** | Conflating a resolved first-try slip with an unresolved second-try failure obscures which points still require caregiver follow-up. | Tracked three separate flags per recovery point in `src/utils/demoState.ts` and displayed all three distinctly in the S8 Caregiver Summary: (1) `hadIncorrectAttempt`, (2) `eventuallyCorrect`, and (3) `needsCaregiverDiscussion` (flagged after a second incorrect answer when the answer is explained). | Only two attempts are tracked per comprehension check before revealing the answer and advancing. | Automated unit tests (`src/utils/demoState.test.ts`) and browser walkthroughs verifying 1st-try correct, 1st-incorrect → revisit → 2nd-try correct, and 2nd-incorrect → explanation + caregiver discussion flag. |

## 4. Accessibility Standards (WCAG 2.2 AA)

| Source | Finding or Recommendation | Design Decision | Limitation | Proposed Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WCAG 2.2 AA — Contrast (1.4.3) & Non-Text Contrast (1.4.11)** | Body text requires $\ge 4.5:1$ contrast and large controls require clear visual boundaries; orange text on cream (`2.1:1`) fails contrast and red error states can increase child anxiety. | Used Warm Ink `#3A2E26` on Warm Cream `#FFF8EC` (`12.4:1`), Deep Teal `#1F6F6B` with white text (`5.9:1`), and Gentle Orange `#F2994A` strictly as a surface fill paired with Warm Ink text (`5.9:1`). Avoided red entirely; used Soft Teal `#DDF1EE` + tick icon for correct states and Soft Orange `#FDE8D4` + retry/discussion icon for incorrect states. | High-contrast dark mode is not included in V0. | Automated contrast audit and colour-blindness simulation verifying that every state is communicated via icon + text label + Kare's expression, never colour alone. |
| **WCAG 2.2 AA — Target Size (2.5.8), Keyboard Accessible (2.1.1), Focus Visible (2.4.7) & Reduced Motion (2.3.3)** | Interactive controls for children and shared tablet/laptop use need large hit targets, full keyboard navigation, visible focus rings, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion`. | Enforced `min-h-[56px]` on primary/navigation buttons (`min-h-[68px]` on comprehension choices and `min-h-[88px]` on face buttons), native `<button>` and `<input type="checkbox">` semantics, `3px solid #1F6F6B` focus-visible rings, and a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` rule in `src/index.css`. | Does not include custom switch-access scanning modes beyond standard `Tab` / `Shift+Tab` / `Enter` / `Space`. | Full keyboard-only walkthrough across Journey 1 and Journey 2 plus viewport checks at `1024×768` and `1366×768`. |

## 5. Working Assumptions & Design Choices Requiring Evaluation

| Source | Finding or Recommendation | Design Decision | Limitation | Proposed Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Design Choice / Assumption A1 & A3 — Target Age Range (7–12) & Single Reading Level** | Co-use by a 7-to-12-year-old child and caregiver is assumed to work with one core reading level supplemented by expandable `"More about this"` detail. | Rendered core child text at `22px–26px` with an optional `"More about this"` disclosure on story pages 1–4 and recovery steps 1–3. | A 7-year-old and a 12-year-old have substantially different reading fluency and informational needs; one tier may feel too hard for 7-year-olds or too simple for 12-year-olds. | Stratified workshop evaluation with younger (7–8) vs. older (10–12) children measuring independent reading ease and how often `"More about this"` is opened. |
| **Design Choice — Mascot Character (Kare the Bear)** | A consistent, friendly animal companion wearing a matching arm cast is hypothesized to model the procedure and reduce fear. | Illustrated Kare as a round honey-brown bear in a deep teal scrub top with an orange left-arm cast across four expressions (`waving`, `calm`, `celebrating`, `encouraging`) and scene props. | Older children (10–12) may perceive a teddy bear mascot as too young for them, or children with leg casts may notice Kare wears an arm cast. | Post-session child interview and optional enjoyment question (`"Did you like learning with Kare?"`) segmented by child age. |
| **Design Choice — Colour Palette Preferences (Warm Cream, Warm Ink, Deep Teal, Gentle Orange)** | Warm, non-clinical colours with zero red error states are hypothesized to feel calmer than sterile white/blue hospital interfaces. | Applied `#FFF8EC`, `#3A2E26`, `#1F6F6B`, and `#F2994A` consistently across all 8 screens. | Subjective aesthetic preference; has not yet been compared against alternative palettes with paediatric patients. | Brief visual preference check with children and caregivers during the prototype workshop. |
| **Design Choice — Sentence-Length Target ($\le 12$ words per child sentence, one idea per sentence)** | Short sentences aim to reduce cognitive load for anxious children. | Kept child-facing sentences short and concrete (`Nunito` $\ge 22\text{px}$, line-height `1.5`), moving extra context into `"More about this"`. | Strict brevity can strip clinical nuance (for example, listing multiple finger warning signs in Step 3 requires 19 words to stay clinically faithful to SickKids guidance). | Read-aloud comprehension check with children aged 7–12 and clinical review of whether any essential nuance was lost in simplification. |
| **Evaluation Protocol Choice — Optional Affect Questions & Natural Error Observation** | Forcing children to answer feelings/enjoyment questions or instructing participants to deliberately make mistakes distorts natural usability behaviour. | Made S3/S8 feelings and enjoyment selectors strictly optional (never blocking navigation), removed `"Pretend Sam gets one answer wrong"` from participant prompts, and verified first/second incorrect branches in developer testing. | Natural workshop sessions may see mostly correct answers on simple 2–3 choice questions, meaning fewer organic observations of the retry flow unless a child genuinely hesitates. | Verify retry and second-incorrect flows deterministically in developer testing (`src/utils/demoState.test.ts` + Playwright), and observe natural participant behaviour unprompted in workshops. |
